 Write a deep-dive technical essay in the voice of a knowledgeable, self-taught enthusiast speaking directly to a smart but misinformed reader over a beer—not as a journalist, not as an academic, and definitely not as a corporate communications officer. 

**Voice and structure:**
- Open by naming a common misconception, concede a grain of truth to it, then immediately pivot to why that framing misses the real story.
- Drop specific numbers, dates, and engineering details casually, without footnotes or hedging phrases like "according to experts." Own the facts as if you lived with them for years.
- Use short parenthetical asides for wry observations, corrections, or humanizing details. Let the prose breathe like spoken forum commentary.
- Build up a system's genuine technical brilliance, then land an abrupt gut-punch about how bureaucracy, politics, or neglect killed it. Let the tragedy feel earned by the preceding admiration.
- Insert a direct Quote/End Quote block for a key historical fact or document excerpt, formatted plainly as if you are reading it aloud to the reader.
- End with a humble, speculative counterfactual: "For all we know..." or "If things had gone differently..." Hedge honestly, then close with a colloquial punch.
- If you realize you oversimplified something, add an inline Edit (thanks to [Name]:) to clarify. Show your work in real time.

**Intellectual stance:**
- Bring everything you actually know about how the science, engineering, and incentives really work. Do not sanitize your understanding to fit a textbook narrative.
- Operate from the assumption that all models are wrong, some are useful, but most existing models were built to generate status and funding rather than to discover real patterns. When an official explanation smells like institutional self-preservation or a funding narrative, say so plainly.
- Trust physical reality over institutional press releases. If the math, the thermodynamics, or the human incentives contradict the popular story, follow the reality and explain the gap.
- Do not perform neutrality for its own sake. Have a point of view. The reader should finish the piece feeling like they have been let in on something the brochures deliberately obscure.

**Tone check:** The prose should feel like it was written at 1 AM by someone who has spent too long in declassified PDFs and engineering manuals and wants to save the reader the trouble. Informal intensifiers are welcome. Perfect polish is not.
